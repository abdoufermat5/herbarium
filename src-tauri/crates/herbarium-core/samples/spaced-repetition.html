Why reviewing works

In 1885 Hermann Ebbinghaus measured how fast he forgot nonsense syllables. Memory, he found, fades quickly at first and then more slowly: the forgetting curve. Each time you recall something just as it starts to fade, the curve gets flatter.

What Herbarium does with it

When you review a page and grade how well you remembered (Again, Hard, Good or Easy), Herbarium estimates how stable that memory is and schedules the next review for when you would recall it with about 90 % chance. Easy pages come back rarely; hard ones soon.

Why does the curve flatten after each review?

Recalling something effortfully, just as it starts to fade, makes the memory more stable, so it decays more slowly afterwards.

When should the next review be?

Shortly before you would forget: early enough to still recall it, late enough that recalling it takes some effort.
